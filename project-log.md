# Project Log

### Log 1 - Initial Ideas

## Activities Completed

- Completed the "Record of Initial Ideas" section of the EPQ production log.
- Refined the project focus from ethical hacking in general to penetration testing.
- Justified the topic choice based on personal interest in cybersecurity and future career aspirations.
- Expanded initial research using academic journals, industry reports, and professional cybersecurity sources.
- Identified key perspectives including technical, legal, ethical, and organisational viewpoints.
- Planned practical investigation through TryHackMe activities.
- Planned analysis of Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report (DBIR) data.
- Developed a framework for comparing common cyber-attack methods against the capabilities of penetration testing.
- Discussed the project with my supervisor and received feedback on research objectives and project structure.

### Reflection

Completing the initial ideas and research planning stage helped me develop a clearer understanding of the direction of my project. While my original idea focused on ethical hacking as a broad topic, further reading highlighted how extensive the field is. This led me to narrow my investigation to penetration testing, which is a more focused and manageable area for an EPQ research essay.

I identified a wide range of sources presenting different viewpoints. Some emphasise penetration testing as one of the most effective tools for identifying vulnerabilities before attackers exploit them, while others highlight limitations such as its point-in-time nature and dependence on organisational follow-up action. This demonstrated that the project would require evaluation rather than simply describing cybersecurity practices.

My supervisor suggested breaking the project into smaller objectives, which helped me create a clearer structure for future research. We also discussed how penetration testing does not directly prevent cyber-attacks but instead identifies weaknesses that companies can address. This discussion reinforced the need to compare penetration testing with other approaches such as continuous monitoring, employee awareness training, and access controls.

Overall, this stage strengthened the scope of my project and provided a clear plan for expanding my research using both academic sources and practical investigation.

### Log 2 - Candidate Proposal (Part A)

### Activities Completed

- Completed the EPQ Candidate Proposal (Part A).
- Finalised the working title: "To what extent is penetration testing the most effective way to protect companies from cyber-attacks?"
- Evaluated a range of academic and industry sources related to penetration testing and ethical hacking.
- Identified key areas for investigation, including effectiveness, limitations, legal considerations, and comparison with other security controls.
- Completed practical penetration-testing activities on TryHackMe.
- Conducted independent analysis of data from the Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report (DBIR).
- Selected common cyber-attack methods from the DBIR and compared them against the capabilities of penetration testing.
- Researched the 2026 Binance cyberattack as a real-world cybersecurity case study.
- Developed a scoring framework to evaluate how effectively penetration testing addresses different attack methods.
- Refined project objectives following supervisor feedback.
  
### Reflection

Completing the Candidate Proposal helped formalise the direction of my EPQ and provided a stronger foundation for future research. The proposal required me to justify my research question, identify suitable sources and explain how I would gather evidence. This process highlighted the importance of balancing practical investigation with academic research.

One of the most valuable developments during this stage was analysing Verizon DBIR data. This allowed me to move beyond theoretical discussions and examine the real causes of cyber incidents. By comparing common attack methods with the types of vulnerabilities penetration testing can uncover, I began developing a more evidence-based assessment of its effectiveness.

I also completed practical TryHackMe activities, which improved my understanding of how penetration testing works in practice. This helped me connect academic theories with real techniques used by security professionals. Researching a recent cybersecurity case study further demonstrated how organisations face a range of threats that cannot always be prevented by a single security measure.

As my research progressed, I became increasingly aware that penetration testing works most effectively when combined with other security controls. While it remains an important method for identifying vulnerabilities, many sources argue that continuous monitoring, employee training and access control policies are also essential. This suggests that my final conclusion may focus on penetration testing as one key component of a wider cybersecurity strategy rather than a standalone solution.
